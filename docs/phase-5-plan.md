## Goal
The closed technical alpha as one coherent product (Master Prompt §2A.1): an invited adult goes from the invitation email through onboarding to today's Daily Drop, answers it, talks with other members under the answers, follows people, keeps a profile and a Music DNA, and can mute, block and report. Staff invite members, run the Daily Drop (calendar, reserve, alerts) and handle reports. Every enabled feature carries its safeguards (§2A.5): authorization through the policy module on every channel, rate limits, audit, data export and deletion, HR/EN, WCAG 2.2 AA.

At the end of Phase 5 the application is **feature-complete for the alpha**; Phase 6 adds operations (backups and a restore drill, monitoring, incident runbook, legal documents) and the gate G-A decides invitations.

### 1.2 RECOMMENDED inside the alpha (Claude's proposals; approved with the plan, D-092)
- **Daily Drop push and the PWA service worker** as the last feature step, behind feature flags, so they can move to early beta without touching anything else (CONFIRMED in the alpha, D-093 (1)).
- **"For You" sort in the alpha** = answers from people the viewer follows first, then the rest by Recent; no scoring, no reactions in the order (§5: ranking must not be reaction counts). "Most Discussed" = number of member-visible comments, ties by Recent; new answers with zero comments are shown after the discussed ones but never hidden (§5 "avoid burying quiet members": the list is complete, only the order changes).
- **Limits (tunable in ADM-17):** answer 1,500 characters, comment 750, bio 200, display name three words (D-085), reports 10 per member per day, follows 60 per hour, answers and comments 30 per hour, invites 50 per admin per day, 5 catalogue references per answer or comment, catalogue typeahead 2 searches per second per member. Rate-limit messages per JR7.
- **Follow requests after a decline:** one new request per pair per 7 days (J4 E6).
- **Public IDs:** random UUIDs (as Phase 4) for every object; no UUIDv7 in URLs, so creation time never leaks (U-01).
- **Unpublished future dates, reserve and drafts** answer "nije dostupno" (U-03).
- **Moderator conflict of interest** (J5 E3): a moderator blocked by the reported member or the reporter cannot take that case; the queue shows "another moderator" instead. Cheap to build, hard to add later.
- **Moderation copy of removed or author-deleted content** kept 90 days for the case, then purged (J5 E9); counsel to confirm the period (B.3).
- **Deletion workflow:** personal data erased or anonymized; answers, comments and reactions deleted; thread placeholders only where other members' replies exist (Master Prompt §3 "do not preserve full posts anonymously"); audit and moderation records kept under the retention policy; the 30-day grace runs as a worker job; a sign-in during the grace period cancels it (D-063 (9)).
- **Data export:** a ZIP of JSON files (profile, settings, Music DNA, answers, comments, reactions, follows, blocks, mutes, reports made, notices received), generated by the worker, downloadable for 24 hours through a signed link, offered inside the delete flow and on request through the privacy contact (D-066).
- **General rate limiter** in PostgreSQL (same pattern as the sign-in throttle), per member and per hashed IP; no new dependency.
- **Service worker written by hand** (no library): app shell, fonts, icons, the last-viewed Daily Drop question; nothing personal (U-11); cleared on sign-out.

## 5. Risks specific to this phase
1. **Size (A-01).** Fourteen steps, each a product area. Mitigation: dependency order, one step per session, flags for anything not finished, no step starts before the previous one's tests are green.
2. **A7 dependency.** Images (5.11), real push tests (5.12) and staging (4.11) need the owner's accounts. Mitigation: everything else is independent; the design works without photos (D-017).
3. **iOS push** needs the PWA on the Home Screen and a real device over HTTPS (T-04); the EU status is partly unverified. Mitigation: push is flagged and the reminder copy already explains installation (D-063 (5)).
4. **MusicBrainz availability and the 1 request per second rule.** Mitigation: server-side queue and cache; every referenced artist is stored locally, so existing DNA never depends on the API; the failure state is designed (D-078).
5. **The Spirala in production.** The prototype's helix (`dna.js`) is the most expensive visual to port; it must be smooth on a mid-range phone and still under reduced motion. Mitigation: SVG with CSS animation only, measured in 5.1, simplified if the frame budget is missed (the owner is told before any visual change).
6. **Deletion and export correctness (GDPR).** Mitigation: a db test that scans every table for the deleted member's id after the purge; counsel's review of the retention periods (B.3).
7. **Moderation records of a one-person team (OQ-06).** Mitigation: the queue works for one person; the alpha notice states the response time honestly (PUB-12); the owner names a backup or the best-effort statement before G-A.
8. **"For You" without a feed.** A thin definition in the alpha (follows first) must not read as broken. Mitigation: the sort is labelled and explained in one line; B1 and B4 enrich it.
9. **The countdown (D-078 (8))** must match the real first publication; the first question is scheduled before any invite goes out (Phase 6 checklist).
10. **Legal placeholders.** Terms, privacy and guidelines are marked as placeholders until counsel; no invite is sent while they are placeholders (acceptance criteria §6).
