## Key law in one place

- **InfoSoc Directive 2001/29/EC, Art. 5(3)(d):** quotation "for purposes such as criticism or review", only for works already lawfully made available, with the source and author named unless impossible, "in accordance with fair practice" and "to the extent required by the specific purpose".
- **CJEU on quotation:** *Pelham* C-476/17, paras 71–74: the user must intend to enter into "dialogue" with the work, and the work must be identifiable. *Spiegel Online* C-516/17, paras 78–80: use "for the purposes of illustrating an assertion, of defending an opinion or of allowing an intellectual comparison", with a "direct and close link" to the user's own reflections; the quoted work plays a secondary role. Para 41: the Art. 5 list is exhaustive. *Funke Medien* C-469/17, para 64: freedom of information cannot justify exceptions beyond Art. 5.
- **CJEU on re-posting:** *Renckhoff* C-161/17, paras 30–35 and 44: copying a photo onto your own server and posting it is a new communication to a new public, even if the photo was freely available elsewhere. A hyperlink is treated differently.
- **ZAPSP (NN 111/21):** čl. 202 (quotation): "doslovno navođenje **ulomaka** autorskog djela (citata) … radi znanstvenog istraživanja, nastave, kritike, polemike, recenzije, osvrta i slično, u mjeri opravdanoj svrhom … i u skladu s dobrim običajima", naming the source and author "ako je to moguće". čl. 181: exceptions only where expressly listed (para 1), plus the three-step test (para 2). čl. 50–53: online content-sharing service providers. čl. 173–178: sui generis right of database makers. čl. 65–67: licence contracts.
- **CC licences:** the 2.0, 3.0 (including the Croatian 3.0 port) and 4.0 texts are cited per question.

## E.1 Album covers under the quotation exception

**Question.** Can album covers be displayed under InfoSoc Art. 5(3)(d) and ZAPSP? Does monetisation change this?

**Short answer.** No, not as catalogue decoration. Showing a cover on every album page or card to identify the record is not a quotation: there is no "dialogue", assertion or comparison (*Pelham*, *Spiegel Online*). A whole cover is also not an "ulomak" (extract) under čl. 202. A cover used inside real criticism (a review that discusses the artwork) can fall under the exception, case by case. Monetisation does not switch the exception off, but it weakens the "fair practice" and three-step-test arguments.

**Analysis.**
- The exception is purpose-bound. Systematic display of every cover is the normal way covers are exploited and licensed (stores, streaming services), so it fails čl. 181(2): it "conflicts with normal exploitation".
- Croatia transposed the exception narrowly ("ulomaka"). The CJEU leaves Member States discretion here (*Funke Medien* para 43), so the Croatian wording matters.
- Copying covers from the Cover Art Archive to our own storage is a new communication to the public (*Renckhoff*). Hotlinking or embedding is also unsafe: CAA images are uploaded by volunteers, not by rightholders, and the archive grants no licence (earlier research, 2026-11-01). Under *GS Media* C-160/15 *(not re-fetched)*, a for-profit linker is presumed to know that the source was unauthorised.
- Each cover can carry several rights (photographer, designer, label), which none of our sources clears.
- Lawful routes: covers supplied by artists or labels under the Terms licence (E.7), a licensed artwork provider, or an official third-party embed whose provider shows its own licensed artwork (an owner decision, since D-017 and the no-native-audio rule apply).

**Sources.** [Directive 2001/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2001/29/oj); [Pelham C-476/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0476); [Spiegel Online C-516/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0516); [Funke Medien C-469/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0469); [Renckhoff C-161/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0161); [ZAPSP NN 111/21](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2021_10_111_1941.html) čl. 181, 202.

**Confidence.** High that catalogue-wide display is not a quotation. Medium for cover images inside member reviews.

**Residual risk.** High if covers were shown on catalogue pages without a licence. Low today, because none are shown (D-017).

**Gap against what is built or decided.** None. D-017 already forbids unlicensed covers, and no covers are built. OQ-29 ideas that rely on covers stay blocked. Member images (D-120/D-121) may contain covers that members photograph or upload. That is a hosting question under E.6 and E.7, not a quotation by GrooveRift.

**Owner decision or action.** None now. Before any cover feature: choose a licensed source (OPEN). RECOMMENDED: keep D-017 unchanged.

**Free official channel.** DZIV (State Intellectual Property Office; it is also the copyright authority) answers general copyright questions: dziv.hr.

## E.2 Artist photos from Wikimedia Commons (D-118)

### Common background

- **Licence elements by version** (legal code read on 2026-11-07):
  - **2.0 (BY-SA 4(a), 4(c)):** credit "reasonable to the medium or means You are utilizing". Name the original author and the work's title if supplied, give the URI the licensor specifies, and keep all copyright notices intact. "Such credit may be implemented in any reasonable manner". For a Derivative Work, add a credit that identifies the use. Include the licence URI "with every copy".
  - **3.0 Unported and the Croatian port 3.0 HR:** the same structure ("u mjeri u kojoj je primjereno za medije ili sredstva koja koristite", "na bilo koji način koji je primjeren"). §3(b) also requires adaptations to "clearly label, demarcate or otherwise identify that changes were made". §7(a) ends the licence **automatically on breach** ("prestat će automatski važiti").
  - **4.0 (§3(a)):** identify the creator, keep notices, link the material "to the extent reasonably practicable", "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material", and give the licence link. §3(a)(2): any reasonable manner "based on the medium, means, and context", for example "a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information". §2(a)(4): technical modifications "never" produce Adapted Material. §6(b): the licence is **reinstated** if a breach is cured within 30 days of discovery.
- **CC FAQ ("How do I properly attribute…"):** for versions before 4.0 "you must also provide the title of the work". Linking to a place with the attribution information is accepted. Modifications are to be indicated, "for example, if you have taken an excerpt, or cropped a photo". Trivial changes need no note. The CC wiki recommends TASL (Title, Author, Source, Licence).
- **Copyleft trolling (Wikimedia Commons page):** some photographers license under CC 2.0/3.0 and then claim money for any imperfect credit. This exploits the termination-without-cure clause; Pixsy does not take 4.0 images. **Many Commons photos of musicians come from Flickr, which offers only CC 2.0**, so this risk is concrete for GrooveRift.
- **German appellate example** (OLG Köln, 31 Oct 2014, 6 U 60/14, CC BY-NC 2.0; reported by netzpolitik.org, a secondary source): cropping a photo was treated as an adaptation (Bearbeitung). Not marking it as one, and cutting off an author notice embedded in the image, breached the licence even though the author, the licence and links were all given. The court said the correct credit was "Ausschnitt eines Fotos von …" ("detail of a photo by …"). Croatia is not bound by this, but CC claims are commonly brought in Germany.
- **What is built** (code read 2026-11-07): `src/lib/catalog/commons.ts` requests only `LicenseShortName|LicenseUrl|Artist`. `artist_photo` stores author, licence, licence URL and file page. `acceptLicence` in `src/lib/media/rules.ts` implements D-118 (3). The "O fotografiji" sheet is not built yet.

### E.2 (a) Is a credit one tap away "reasonable to the medium" for CC 2.0 and 3.0, not only 4.0?

**Short answer.** Probably yes on the artist page, where the "i" button sits on the photo, with medium confidence for 2.0/3.0 and high confidence for 4.0. The weak points are elsewhere: (1) the **content** of the credit (title, a note that the photo was cropped, the exact licence version and port); (2) **small tiles** that show 2.0/3.0 photos with no credit and no licence link, which the 2.0/3.0 texts require "with every copy".

**Analysis.**
- 2.0 and 3.0 contain no thumbnail or link-only safe harbour. They do let the reuser choose "any reasonable manner" suited to the medium. A control placed on the image, labelled "O fotografiji" and opening the full credit and links in one action, is a common web pattern and is close to Wikipedia's own practice (the image opens a viewer with the credit). CC's FAQ accepts linking to attribution information.
- Against this: (i) 2.0/3.0 terminate automatically on breach, with no cure period; (ii) German courts read CC conditions strictly (OLG Köln). The one German "mouseover is not enough" ruling (AG Düsseldorf, 3 Sep 2014, 57 C 5593/14, secondary report) concerned Pixelio's own terms, not CC.
- **Prominence clause:** for a Derivative or Collective Work, the credit must appear "where any other comparable authorship credit appears" and be at least as prominent. If the artist page ever shows other visible authorship credits (for example "Podaci: MusicBrainz" or member bylines on content), the photo credit should not be less prominent than those. Keep page-level credits in the same "i" pattern or make the photo credit equally visible.
- **Tiles (D-118 (2) and (4)):** a 40 to 64 px copy in search, Music DNA or answer cards links to the artist page, where the credit is one more tap away. For 4.0 this is arguably a hyperlink "to a resource that includes the required information". For 2.0/3.0 it is the most attackable point: each tile is a copy shown without credit or licence URI.

**Sources.** [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/legalcode); [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/legalcode); [CC BY-SA 3.0 HR](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/hr/legalcode); [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en); [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/); [CC recommended practices](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Recommended_practices_for_attribution); [Commons: Credit line](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Credit_line); [Commons: Copyleft trolling](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyleft_trolling); [netzpolitik.org on OLG Köln 6 U 60/14](https://netzpolitik.org/2014/der-dritte-streich-olg-koeln-zu-deutschlandradios-nutzung-cc-lizenzierter-fotos-mit-nc-modul/) (secondary).

**Confidence.** High for 4.0 on the artist page. Medium for 2.0/3.0 on the artist page. Low to medium that uncredited tiles are compliant for 2.0/3.0.

**Residual risk.** Medium for 2.0/3.0 photos: a demand letter (typically a few hundred euros per image in German practice; secondary knowledge) and loss of the licence until a new grant. Low for 4.0, CC0 and PD.

**Gap against what is built or decided.**
- The credit lacks the **title** (required for ≤3.0 "if supplied"), the **modification note**, the licensor-specified **attribution text** (extmetadata `Attribution`), and any **credit or copyright line** (`Credit`). None of these is fetched today.
- Tiles show 2.0/3.0 photos without credit (D-118 (2)).

**Owner decision or action.**

- **Needs owner survey** (changes D-118 (2)/(4)). Tiles for photos under CC 2.0/2.5/3.0 (any port):
  - (A) keep as decided and accept the risk;
  - (B) RECOMMENDED: show 2.x/3.0 photos only on the artist page with the "i" credit, and use the generated tile in small tiles. PD, CC0 and 4.0 photos appear everywhere as decided;
  - (C) as (B), and also an always-visible one-line credit under 2.x/3.0 photos on the artist page.

**Free official channel.** Creative Commons publishes the FAQ (no individual advice). For a specific photo, the uploader's Commons talk page can confirm the preferred credit in writing, which is useful evidence.

### E.2 (b) Does the circle crop make us distribute an adaptation under BY-SA, and what must we state?

**Short answer.** Treat it as an adaptation. Resizing and re-encoding are technical modifications and are not adaptations (4.0 §2(a)(4); 3.0 §3, last sentence). Cropping to a framed circle is a content choice. CC's FAQ names "cropped a photo" as a modification to indicate, and OLG Köln treated a crop as an adaptation. The consequences are small:
1. say that the photo was cropped (and resized);
2. for 2.0/3.0, credit in the form "detail (crop) of a photo by X";
3. the cropped file is under the **same BY-SA licence** (same version, or a later one with the same elements);
4. GrooveRift adds no terms that restrict reuse of that file.

ShareAlike does **not** extend to the artist page, the app or other content. A page that places the image next to other material is at most a Collection, which 3.0 §1(a) excludes from "Adaptation".

**Analysis.**
- Under 4.0, "Adapted Material" exists only where a change needs permission under copyright law. EU law does not harmonise the adaptation right for photos. In Croatia, whether a mechanical crop is a "prerada" is unsettled. The cautious reading costs one sentence, so it is the sensible one.
- CC BY (without SA) also requires the modification note (4.0 §3(a)(1)(B); 3.0 §3(b) labelling; 2.0 credit for the Derivative Work). Only the "same licence" consequence is specific to BY-SA.
- **Embedded notices:** a crop must not cut off a watermark or a name printed in the image. 2.0/3.0 require that all copyright notices be kept intact, and this was the second breach in OLG Köln.
- The Terms (E.7, F) must exclude third-party licensed photos from any "all rights reserved" or no-reuse clause. 3.0 §4(a) forbids terms that restrict recipients' rights under the licence.

**Sources.** As E.2 (a); [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/) ("What is an adaptation?", "When is my use considered an adaptation?", "How do I properly attribute…").

**Confidence.** Medium on whether a crop is legally an adaptation in Croatia. High that the stated wording below satisfies every version either way.

**Residual risk.** Low once the note and the licence line are shown.

**Gap against what is built or decided.** D-118 (2) lists author, licence and "Wikimedia Commons", with no modification note and no statement that the crop is under the same licence. The 512 px square crop "towards the upper third" can cut off embedded credits.

**What the sheet must state** (Croatian UI text is RECOMMENDED wording, to be fixed in 5.13 copy):
- CC BY / BY-SA: "Fotografija je izrezana i smanjena." (EN: "Cropped and resized from the original.")
- 2.0/3.0, in the credit itself: "Izrez fotografije: ‹autor›" (EN: "Detail of a photo by ‹author›").
- BY-SA only: "Ova izrezana verzija dostupna je pod istom licencom (‹CC BY-SA x.y›)." with the licence link.
- PD and CC0: the note is optional; RECOMMENDED for consistency.

**Owner decision or action.** None beyond accepting the build list (no change to D-118).

**Free official channel.** As E.2 (a).

### E.2 (c) Is "Prijavi problem" to the DSA notice form plus an admin "hide photo" an adequate takedown path?

**Short answer.** Yes as an intake and remedy channel, with four additions:
1. For these photos GrooveRift is the **publisher**, not a host. We pick, copy and display the image ourselves, so the DSA hosting exemption (Art. 6, which covers information provided by a recipient of the service) does not shield us, and liability does not wait for a notice. Speed and evidence therefore matter more than form.
2. A **wrong-person photo** engages personality rights (ZOO čl. 19: "ime, privatnost osobnog i obiteljskog života" and the open "i dr."; Constitution čl. 35) and GDPR accuracy (Art. 5(1)(d), Art. 16). It should be hidden **on receipt**, before verification.
3. A photographer's copyright claim (for example licence laundering on Commons) should also be hidden on receipt and the licence evidence checked.
4. Hiding should remove the **file from R2 and any cache**, not only the database link.

**Analysis.**
- Croatian personality rights: ZOO čl. 19(2) lists protected rights non-exhaustively; non-pecuniary damage for their violation is in ZOO čl. 1100 *(not re-fetched)*. For a public figure, an informational photo on a page about that person is a low-risk use (ECtHR *Von Hannover (No. 2)* and *Axel Springer* balancing, *not re-fetched*). The risk rises with a wrong person, private-setting photos, minors in group shots, or any advertising use that suggests endorsement. Commons itself warns that "commercial use of images of people may require the explicit agreement of the subject". Croatia is not listed in Commons' country consent table.
- Copyright claims: a CC licence binds only if the uploader was the author. Keeping a **snapshot of the licence evidence** (file page URL, licence, author, revision timestamp, retrieval date) supports good faith and helps answer a claim quickly. Under ZOO, fault is presumed for damages; acting at once limits exposure.
- GDPR aspects (lawful basis for publishing artists' photos, objection under Art. 21) belong to section C; they are cross-referenced, not decided here.

**Sources.** [ZOO NN 35/05, čl. 19](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2005_03_35_707.html) (consolidated text on zakon.hr, in force version from 7 Jul 2026); [Commons: Reusing content outside Wikimedia](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia); [Commons: Country specific consent requirements](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Country_specific_consent_requirements); [CC BY-SA 4.0 §2(b)(1)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en) (publicity and personality rights not licensed).

**Confidence.** Medium-high.

**Residual risk.** Low for correct photos of public artists. Medium for wrong-person photos until they are hidden.

**Gap against what is built or decided.**
- D-118 (5) has no report-reason choice, no hide-on-receipt rule, no file deletion and no licence snapshot.
- "No member 'wrong person' button" (D-118 (5)) is fine, provided the notice form offers that reason.

**Owner decision or action.** RECOMMENDED, within D-118 (5), no survey needed:
- notice-form reasons "Nije ova osoba / Not this person", "Ja sam autor/autorica fotografije / I took this photo", "Pogrešan ili nepotpun potpis / Wrong or missing credit", "Drugo";
- the first two hide the photo automatically until review;
- the target response time is short (for example 48 h) and stated in the copyright policy (F).

**Free official channel.** AZOP (azop.hr) for the data-protection side; DZIV for general copyright information.

## E.3 Storing Spotify, Apple and Deezer IDs or URLs from MusicBrainz

**Question.** Does storing these IDs and URLs (taken from MusicBrainz URL relationships) breach those services' terms or EU database rights?

**Short answer.** No breach found, with high-medium confidence.
- **Terms:** the Spotify Developer Terms bind those who "use or otherwise access the Spotify Platform" (APIs, SDKs, widgets). GrooveRift does not, so their "no databases of Spotify Content" clause does not reach us. The same logic applies to Apple and Deezer.
- **Database right:** MusicBrainz relationships and URLs are **core data under CC0**, and CC0 waives sui generis database rights too. Spotify's own catalogue right (Directive 96/9/EC Art. 7) is not infringed by a few identifiers per artist that volunteers entered one by one. That is not a "substantial part", and it is not repeated, systematic extraction (Art. 7(5)) of Spotify's database. Under *British Horseracing Board* C-203/02 *(not re-fetched)*, investment in **creating** data such as IDs does not count.
- **Linking:** a plain link to freely available content is not a communication to a new public (*Svensson* C-466/12, *not re-fetched*).

**Analysis.** ZAPSP čl. 173–178 transposes the database right: extraction or re-utilisation of all or a substantial part, 15 years. The risk would arise if GrooveRift **fetched data from Spotify, Apple or Deezer by those IDs** (titles, artwork, popularity), or used the Spotify API for an import. From then on the Developer Terms would apply to everything obtained ("Do not store Spotify Content indefinitely"; "create compilations or databases of Spotify Content" is restricted).

**Sources.** [MusicBrainz Database (core vs. supplementary)](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_Database); [MusicBrainz data licence](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/About/Data_License); [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode); [Spotify Developer Terms v10](https://developer.spotify.com/terms); [Directive 96/9/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1996/9/oj); ZAPSP čl. 173–178.

**Confidence.** High that we are not party to their developer terms. Medium-high on the database right.

**Residual risk.** Low.

**Gap against what is built or decided.** None for IDs. RECOMMENDED guardrail: no request to Spotify, Apple or Deezer endpoints, and no scraping of their pages by stored IDs. If an optional Spotify import is ever built, keep its data separately with its provenance.

**Owner decision or action.** None.

**Free official channel.** None needed.

## E.4 Service names and logos on "listen on" buttons without API agreements

**Short answer.** **Names in plain text are fine**; logos and badges are not, except as described here. Using "Spotify", "Apple Music" or "Deezer" to say where a link leads is referential use permitted by trademark law (EUTMR Art. 14(1)(c); Zakon o žigu čl. 16(1) t. 3 and (2): use "u svrhu prepoznavanja ili upućivanja", "u skladu s dobrom poslovnom praksom"), provided it suggests no affiliation (*Gillette* C-228/03, *BMW* C-63/97, *not re-fetched*). Logos are governed by each owner's brand terms:
- **Apple** publishes "Listen on Apple Music" badges for "linking to artists, albums, songs or playlists on Apple Music". The badge must not be modified, must come first when shown with other services' badges, and must not imply endorsement. Apple's identity guidelines also give a trademark line ("Apple and Apple Music are trademarks of Apple Inc. …").
- **Spotify:** its design page states that using its brand resources means accepting the Developer Terms. Those terms allow Spotify Marks "solely to promote your use" of the platform and forbid suggesting partnership or endorsement.
- **Deezer:** its brand site (deezerbrand.com) could not be read; terms unverified.

**Analysis.** Plain-text buttons ("Slušaj na Spotifyju ↗") plus a neutral external-link icon carry the least risk and need no agreement. Using the Spotify logo without accepting its Developer Terms is the riskiest option; accepting those terms just for a logo would bring obligations without benefit.

**Sources.** [Regulation (EU) 2017/1001](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/1001/oj) Art. 14; [Zakon o žigu NN 14/19](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2019_02_14_272.html) čl. 16 (consolidated text on [zakon.hr](https://www.zakon.hr/z/120/Zakon-o-%C5%BEigu), unamended); [Apple Music Identity Guidelines](https://marketing.services.apple/apple-music-identity-guidelines); [Spotify Design & Branding Guidelines](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design); [Spotify Developer Terms v10](https://developer.spotify.com/terms).

**Confidence.** High for plain-text names. Medium for the Apple badge without an Apple partnership (the guidelines address marketers generally). Low for Deezer (unverified).

**Residual risk.** Low with text only.

**Gap against what is built or decided.** No decision on the button design was found in DECISIONS.md.

**Owner decision or action.** RECOMMENDED: text buttons without logos. A footer or Help line: "Spotify, Apple Music i Deezer žigovi su njihovih vlasnika; GrooveRift nije povezan s njima." If the owner wants logos: the Apple badge only per Apple's guidelines; no Spotify logo; Deezer only after reading its terms (OPEN).

**Free official channel.** None; the brand owners' own guideline pages.

## E.5 MetaBrainz commercial tier: obligations and timing

**Short answer.**
- **Data:** core data is CC0, so the data licence imposes no legal obligation.
- **API:** the web service is a separate matter. "Non-commercial use of this web service is free"; commercial users are pointed to the supporter plans. The **Stealth Start-Up** tier ($0/month "and up") is for start-ups "not yet publicly offering services"; MetaBrainz will "keep your data-usage confidential and contact you to discuss your plans". **Bronze** ($100/month "and up") is for "small to mid-size start-ups with public products".
- **Timing:** the invite-only alpha fits Stealth. A public beta, public launch or any paid tier should move to Bronze, as D-018 already budgets. No signed contract text or payment-timing rule was published; the Social Contract is "not a legally binding contract".
- **New finding:** supplementary data (tags, including genre associations, ratings, annotations) is **CC BY-NC-SA 3.0**, and the built app uses it (see the gap below).

**Analysis.** The relationship rests on MetaBrainz's published plans and goodwill, not on a published contract. The real obligations are the API rules: at most 1 request per second, and a meaningful User-Agent (D-092/D-108 already cover the contact). An alternative that does not depend on the API is a mirror built from the CC0 core **dumps**. The Live Data Feed replication packets are themselves CC BY-NC-SA, which again points to a commercial arrangement.

**Sources.** [MetaBrainz supporter tiers](https://metabrainz.org/supporters/account-type); [Social Contract](https://metabrainz.org/social-contract); [MusicBrainz API](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API); [Data licence](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/About/Data_License); [MusicBrainz Database](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_Database).

## E.6 Are we an online content-sharing service provider (DSM Art. 17 / ZAPSP)? Short videos with background music

**Short answer.** Very probably **not**, now or in the beta.
- DSM Art. 2(6) and ZAPSP čl. 50(2) cover services whose **main or one of the main purposes** is to store and give the public access to **a large amount** of protected works uploaded by users, which the service **organises and promotes for profit**. Recital 62 targets services that compete with streaming services for the same audiences.
- GrooveRift is an invite-only discussion community: text answers, one image per post, no audio. It is free, small, and its member content is not public to visitors (D-022).
- Even if it qualified, the lighter regime applies for the first three years while turnover is below €10M (DSM Art. 17(6); ZAPSP čl. 51(3)), with an extra duty above 5 million monthly unique visitors (čl. 51(4)).
- **Consequence:** the ordinary hosting regime applies. Member uploads are covered by the DSA Art. 6 hosting exemption if we act expeditiously on notices (Art. 16 mechanism; section B covers this).

**Videos with background music.** Not in the alpha: D-121 allows still images only, and "no animation kept". If short video is added later (Master Prompt), recorded music in a member's video involves three sets of rights: musical works (HDS ZAMP), phonograms (ZAPRAF) and performances (HUZIP). As a non-OCSSP host, GrooveRift needs no licence but must prohibit unlicensed music in the Terms and remove it on notice. If it ever became an OCSSP, ZAPSP čl. 50(4) requires authorisation, and čl. 217–218 route platform licences for music through collective management. A ZAMP licence then becomes the practical route. Users keep the quotation, criticism, review, caricature, parody and pastiche exceptions (DSM Art. 17(7); ZAPSP čl. 52(5), 202, 206).

**Sources.** [Directive (EU) 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790) Art. 2(6), 17(6)–(8), recitals 62–63; [ZAPSP](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2021_10_111_1941.html) čl. 50–53, 217–218.

**Confidence.** High for the alpha and beta as designed. Medium for a future public feed with video.

**Residual risk.** Low.

**Gap against what is built or decided.** None for the alpha. The Terms and the copyright policy (F) must ban uploading others' covers, photos or music without rights, and describe notice and removal.

**Owner decision or action.** Reassess before any video feature or public feed (DEFERRED). No ZAMP licence now (RECOMMENDED).

## E.7 Content licence wording in the Terms (members keep ownership; limited licence to GrooveRift)

**Short answer.** The model works under Croatian law if the clause is **express and specific**. ZAPSP requires:
- the licence contract in writing (čl. 65; electronic conclusion is permitted under the e-Commerce Directive Art. 9, transposed in ZET);
- that the contract at least name the work and the **manner of use** (čl. 66(1));
- an **express statement that the licence is royalty-free**; otherwise the author may claim "primjerenu i razmjernu naknadu" (čl. 66(1), 67(1)–(2));
- the defaults otherwise apply: **non-exclusive and limited to Croatia** (čl. 58(5)), and doubts are read **in the author's favour** (čl. 58(6));
- the right to be named as author cannot be waived (čl. 28, 64).

**RECOMMENDED clause elements** (drafted in F):
- the member keeps all rights;
- a **non-exclusive, royalty-free, worldwide** licence, limited to operating GrooveRift;
- the uses: store, reproduce, technically adapt (resize, re-encode, crop for display, strip EXIF), show to the audience the member chose, include in notifications and share previews per the visibility settings, and keep backups;
- sub-licensing only to processors that run the service (hosting, storage);
- no use in advertising or marketing without separate consent;
- the licence ends when the member deletes the content or the account, except backup rotation, legal holds and the D-107 moderation evidence (90 days);
- the member warrants that they have the rights, including from people shown in images (D-120);
- the "human-created content" rule;
- a carve-out stating that third-party material (Commons photos, MusicBrainz data) stays under its own licence.

**Sources.** [ZAPSP](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2021_10_111_1941.html) čl. 28, 56, 58, 64–67; [Directive 2000/31/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2000/31/oj) Art. 9.

**Confidence.** Medium-high.

**Residual risk.** Low.

**Gap against what is built or decided.** The Terms are not drafted yet (F). Member images (D-121) need the "faces of others" warranty and the technical-adaptation clause.

**Owner decision or action.** Approve the elements when F is drafted.

## E.8 Trademark "GrooveRift": clearance and filing strategy

**Short answer.**
- **Route:** file an **EU trade mark (EUTM)** at EUIPO, which covers Croatia and all EU states. A national DZIV filing makes sense only if the budget is very tight and the EU market is not intended.
- **Classes:** 9, 41, 42 and 45 as the core; 38 optional.
- **EUIPO official fees** (EUTMR Annex I): **€850** for the first class (e-filing), **€50** for the second, **€150** for each further class. Core four classes = **€1,200**; with class 38 = **€1,350**.
- **SME Fund 2026:** reimburses up to 75% of trade mark fees. It must be approved **before** filing, the call runs until 4 December 2026 (date per secondary sources), and the applicant must be an SME, which depends on the legal entity (OQ-05, section A).
- **Main clearance risk:** "RIFT" (gamigo, EUTM 010026284, classes 9, 16 and 41). With "GROOVE" weak for music services, "RIFT" may be seen as the distinctive element (*Medion* C-120/04, *not re-fetched*).

**Analysis.**
- Before filing, re-run TMview for live "RIFT" marks covering the EU or HR in classes 9, 38, 41, 42 and 45, and record status and owners. The earlier search's Q6 was not completed.
- In an opposition, a mark registered for more than five years can be challenged for proof of genuine use (EUTMR Art. 47(2)). Check whether gamigo's RIFT is used for the registered goods and services.
- The procedure: filing, examination, publication, a three-month opposition period, then registration for 10 years.
- DZIV national fees could not be verified in euros: the fee pages were unreachable and the statute found is the 2000 kuna tariff. DZIV publishes the current schedule.
- Owning the domain gives no trademark right.

**Sources.** [Regulation (EU) 2017/1001](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/1001/oj) Annex I, Art. 46–47; fee figures cross-checked with a national office table updated August 2026 (secondary); [EU IP Helpdesk: SME Fund 2026](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/applications-are-open-2026-sme-fund-2026-02-10_en); `docs/legal/trademark-preliminary-search.md`.

## Build requirements: artist photo credit sheet (step 5.11, D-118)

Items 1 to 12 fit within D-118 as decided. Item 13 needs an owner survey.

1. **Fetch more metadata.** Request `LicenseShortName|LicenseUrl|Artist|Attribution|AttributionRequired|Credit|ObjectName|Restrictions|UsageTerms|DateTime` from `extmetadata`, plus the file's `timestamp` and `sha1` from imageinfo.
2. **Store a licence snapshot per photo.** Keep the file name, the file page URL, the author (plain text), the licensor's attribution text (if any), the title, the licence short name **with version and port** (e.g. "CC BY-SA 3.0 DE"), the licence URL, the Commons revision timestamp/sha1, the retrieval date and a `modified = true` flag. This is the evidence for any claim.
3. **The sheet "O fotografiji" shows**, in this order:
   - the **author** (the licensor's `Attribution` text verbatim if present, otherwise `Artist`), linked to the Commons file page;
   - the **title** (ObjectName, or the file name without extension), required for ≤3.0;
   - the **licence name with version and port**, linked to the licence deed or legal code;
   - "Wikimedia Commons" linked to the file page;
   - the **modification note** "Fotografija je izrezana i smanjena.";
   - for 2.x/3.0, the credit reads "Izrez fotografije: ‹autor›";
   - for BY-SA, "Ova izrezana verzija dostupna je pod istom licencom (‹licence›)." with the link;
   - any `Credit` or copyright line from Commons;
   - "Prijavi problem".
4. **PD and CC0:** show the author if known, the status ("Javno vlasništvo" / "CC0"), the Commons link and the same modification note. Treat US-government "PD-USGov" files as PD only with caution (their status outside the US is uncertain); RECOMMENDED: include them, but list them in the admin view.
5. **Never cut off embedded notices.** Skip files in Commons watermark categories or with a visible credit in the frame (use the `Restrictions` or category check). If a crop would remove a notice, use the generated tile.
6. **Accessibility** (already in D-118): button ≥ 24 px named "O fotografiji", keyboard and screen-reader operable, focus returns to the button. Links are plain text, never third-party HTML (`plainText`/`safeLicenceUrl` already do this).
7. **Licence filter** as built (`acceptLicence`). Additionally reject when `AttributionRequired` is true but no author can be extracted.
8. **Refresh (30 days)** removes our copy when the Commons file is deleted, its licence leaves the allowlist, P18 changes or the author or licence changes. The new snapshot replaces the old; the old stays in the log.
9. **"Prijavi problem"** opens PUB-11 with the artist and the photo ID filled in, and offers the reasons "Nije ova osoba", "Ja sam autor/autorica fotografije", "Pogrešan ili nepotpun potpis" and "Drugo". The first two **hide the photo at once** pending review.
10. **Admin "Sakrij fotografiju"** deletes the R2 object and purges caches (not only a flag), logs the reason and keeps the snapshot. A hidden photo is never refetched (as decided).
11. **No artist photo outside the artist context:** not in Open Graph images, push notifications, emails or GrooveRift marketing. Use the generated tile there.
12. **Public "Izvori fotografija" page** (RECOMMENDED) listing every photo in use with the full credit, linked from the sheet and Help. The Terms carve third-party licensed photos out of GrooveRift's own rights clause.
